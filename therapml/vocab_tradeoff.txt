Vocabulary Size vs. Sequence Length Trade-off

V = Vocabulary
L = Sequence Length

1. Inverse Relationship:
   - Larger Vocabulary means Shorter sequence length
   - Smaller Vocabulary means Longer sequence length

2. Larger Vocab (V high, L low):
   - Pros: Shorter sequences reduce O(L^2) self-attention compute and KV-cache memory; faster generation.
   - Cons: Larger embedding and LM-head matrices (O(V * d_model)); rare tokens receive fewer gradient updates.

3. Smaller Vocab (V low, L high):
   - Pros: Compact embedding tables; all tokens are seen frequently during training.
   - Cons: Exploding sequence lengths inflate quadratic attention costs, increase latency, and shrink the effective context window.

4. Practical Trade-off:
   Compression gains show diminishing returns once frequent subwords are merged. Modern LLMs choose V between 32k and 128k to balance sequence length reduction with embedding memory overhead.
